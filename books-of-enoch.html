The Books of Enoch

Three distinct ancient writings share Enoch's name. This guide explains their origins, subjects and differing religious status.

1 Enoch

Preserved in full in Geʽez, 1 Enoch contains visions, teachings about heavenly beings, judgment and righteousness. The proposed GOD4.us English collection uses R. H. Charles’s 1917 translation, not a new translation made by GOD4.us.

Its five major literary divisions are:

  1. Book of the Watchers (chapters 1–36): heavenly beings, their transgression and judgment.
  2. Book of Parables or Similitudes (37–71): visions of judgment and a heavenly figure.
  3. Astronomical Book or Book of Luminaries (72–82): heavenly lights, calendars and time.
  4. Book of Dream Visions (83–90): symbolic visions of the flood and human history.
  5. Epistle of Enoch (91–108): exhortations, warnings and hope for the righteous; this broad chapter grouping includes appended material.

2 Enoch

Also called the Book of the Secrets of Enoch, this separate work survives principally in Slavonic manuscripts. It recounts Enoch’s journey through the heavens, revelations about creation and teachings to his descendants. It is presented here for historical study, not as Ethiopian Orthodox canonical Scripture.

3 Enoch

Also known as Sefer Hekhalot, this later Hebrew mystical work explores heavenly palaces and the angel Metatron, with whom Enoch is identified in its narrative. It is likewise a related historical text, not part of the Ethiopian Orthodox biblical canon.

Collection status: The proposed 84-entry GOD4.us English collection documents 1 Enoch, but does not currently include 2 Enoch or 3 Enoch. These descriptions are educational only; none of the three texts has been enabled in the public Reader.
